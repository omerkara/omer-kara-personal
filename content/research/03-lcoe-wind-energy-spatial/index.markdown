## Details
In this study, we examine a simple question: **how are macro-level economic, social, environmental, and institutional conditions associated with onshore wind LCOE in Europe, and do these relationships extend beyond national borders?**

We use a balanced annual panel of **10 OECD European countries from 2011 to 2023** and estimate a **Spatial Durbin Model (SDM) with two-way fixed effects**. The spatial framework allows us to distinguish between **domestic relationships** and **cross-border spatial spillovers**, rather than treating countries as independent units.

Our results point to several distinct patterns:

- Stronger **economic performance** is associated with lower onshore wind LCOE, both domestically and through spatial spillovers.
- Stronger **institutional performance** is also associated with lower LCOE through both channels.
- Higher **emission intensity** is associated with higher LCOE through domestic and spatial spillover channels.
- Higher **human development** is associated with higher LCOE primarily through the domestic channel.

An important result is that the spatial dimension matters. For economic and institutional performance, the estimated **indirect impacts are larger than the corresponding direct impacts**, indicating economically relevant cross-country spillovers. Overall, our findings suggest that policies aimed at reducing onshore wind costs in Europe should consider **regional coordination and cross-country interactions**, rather than focusing exclusively on national conditions.

We are also pleased to make all data and code used in the study publicly available, making the analysis fully reproducible.
